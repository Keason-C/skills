**Q16:** Yes, (a), with one correction for zero-delay retries.

When the computed delay is 0, the task is put back in the queue right *after* the `retry` event, not before it. So inside a `retry` listener the task always shows `"retrying"`, whatever the delay. With delay 0, it becomes `"queued"` immediately after the `retry` listeners return. That moment also sets its place in the ready order. No timer is created.

Everything else in (a) stands:
- The task's own outcome, the cascaded cancellations and the freed slot are all committed before any event fires.
- Dispatch happens after those events and emits its own `start` events.
- In a `start` listener the task shows `"running"`.
